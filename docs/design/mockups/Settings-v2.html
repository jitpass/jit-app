Settings · review and redesign · 25 Sep 2026

Settings that fit without a scroll

The window today spends a third of its height on a header and a toolbar, then scrolls to show three cards. This redesign keeps every setting and every sentence that matters, stays on the Windows page's tokens, and opens each tab at the height of what it holds.

What is wrong today

Nine findings, each against a rule we already wrote

  1. It scrolls at the size it opens. Title bar, header and toolbar take about 150 of the 400 points. The App tab's three cards need more than the 250 left. Windows: "A window opens at the height of its content."
  2. The header says little and costs 70 points. The title bar already says Settings. The green mark repeats the menu bar. "The two jit owns restart the service" names two settings without saying which ones.
  3. Every card has two headings. An eyebrow ("Updates") and then a sentence ("This build, and the jit beside it"). The pill already names the tab, so a reader scanning for Updates reads prose instead.
  4. Dots that say nothing. Every card eyebrow carries a green or grey dot. The Windows page drops the all-green dots from pills for exactly this reason.
  5. Destructive buttons live on everyday tabs. A red "Delete in Terminal…" sits on Protection, the tab Settings opens on, under the lock timer.
  6. Notifications are filed under Protection, but they cover scans and AI jobs too. One switch also covers two unrelated events: a session expiring, and a scan finding something new.
  7. A whole row for a healthy fact. "jit is on your PATH" has nothing to do and takes a row. The path only matters when it is the wrong jit.
  8. Row facts are paragraphs. Most run two sentences and wrap to two or three lines. The Windows page asks for one clause.
  9. Popup values are lowercase: "the terminal you are using", "the system default". Status values are Sentence case.

The redesign

Five tabs, no header, one card each where one card is enough

The header goes; the title bar names the window. Notifications get their own tab, and the three things that delete or remove get a tab called Reset, so nothing red sits beside a daily switch. A tab with one card drops the card's eyebrow, because the pill above already says it. The dashed line marks the 400-point opening height.

Decisions for you

Six calls before I build it

  1. Five tabs, with a Reset tab. Recommend: yes. The alternative keeps Vault at the bottom of Protection and Remove at the bottom of General, and both tabs scroll again.
  2. Split "a session expires, or a scan finds something new" into two switches. Recommend: yes. It needs a second preference key. Both new switches start at the old switch's value, so nobody's setting changes on upgrade.
  3. Drop the header. Recommend: yes. The Windows page makes every region but the body optional, and Settings has nothing to count. "jit is not running" moves to the first row of Protection.
  4. Fold the healthy PATH row into the version line. Recommend: yes. The path shows again, with a button, the moment it is the wrong jit or none.
  5. Keep the title bar as "Settings". Recommend: keep. Many macOS apps retitle the window per tab ("General"). The Windows page names the surface, and the selected pill already shows the tab.
  6. The vault key goes in Protection, as one row, not a Vault card. Recommend: yes. It is where the other vault settings are, and it keeps that tab to one card. The Secure Enclave mockup's sheet, alert and Doctor cards stay as drawn; only the "Settings › Vault" wording in its promises becomes "Settings › Protection".

Also changed, with no decision needed: popup values in Sentence case ("System default", not "the system default"); row names shortened so no two start with the same words; "Exclude a Folder…" becomes "Add Folder…" on the card titled for the list it adds to.
