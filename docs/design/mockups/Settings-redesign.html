Settings, rebuilt on the window system.

Today Settings is a 480×420 macOS TabView of grouped Form sections, and every explanation in it is a hover tooltip. Below it is a size-win-s window: one inset, three segments, cards holding rows, and every sentence the tooltips were hiding on screen where it is read.

what opens

520 wide, 400 tall, three segments in the toolbar

The system's table puts Settings at size-win-s: one purpose, one column. The header says the one thing that changes how you read the rest — there is no Save button, and two of these settings are jit's, not the app's. Under it the toolbar splits seven cards three ways, so the window opens on what protects you rather than on a scroll.

the three segments

Seven cards, split where the question changes

The three macOS tabs become three segments of one body. Protection is what jit hands out and what it tells you, down to emptying the vault; Scan is the one group that reads your disk; App is how JitPass behaves on this Mac and how it leaves. Each card keeps its identity in an eyebrow: a dot and a word for its state, a title saying what the group decides, a note only where the cost is not obvious. The sentences under each row are the tooltips, on screen.

what changes
  • window520 wide, opening at the height of its content. Today it is a fixed 480×420, a width that is none of the three in the system's table. Settings is the table's own example of size-win-s: one purpose, nothing to filter.
  • tabsThree macOS tabs become three segments of one body. A tab strip is not one of the six regions; the segmented filter is, and it sits in the toolbar the system already has. Seven cards are two and a half screens at 520×400, and the split puts the group you came for one click from the top rather than one scroll down. Each group still keeps its identity in a card's eyebrow.
  • filterA pill carries the dot of the cards behind it. The one cost of filtering is that a card needing you can sit in a segment you are not looking at. An 8px dot in the worst state of that segment, beside the pill's word, is what buys it back — and it is the one piece drawn here that the system does not have yet.
  • tooltipsEleven .help() tooltips become sentences on screen. Every explanation of what a switch costs currently waits for a hover nobody performs. The row's second line is where the system puts exactly that fact.
  • headerA header the window never had. The service mark, the name, and the one thing that changes how the rest is read: changes apply as you make them, and two of these settings belong to jit, so they restart the service.
  • groupsGrouped Form sections become cards with an eyebrow. A dot and one word carry the group's state, so a group waiting on macOS says so at the top of its own card rather than in grey text further down.
  • conditionsThe conditional notes become rows. Notifications off, never asked, Full Disk Access missing, jit not on PATH: a dot, the word, one fact, and the single button that changes it, inside the card it belongs to.
  • outcomesettingsMessage becomes the banner, and a failure keeps jit's words. A bare line under the form becomes the banner region for what just happened; a refusal becomes a row with a sentence that translates it and jit's own line in an app-verbatim block under it.
  • busy"Applying…" moves onto the row being applied. One spinner under the whole form told you something was happening but not to what. The row spins, its control is disabled, and the rest of the window stays live.
  • excluded"none" becomes "Every folder in your home is scanned". A list says what is true, not that it is empty. The link-styled Remove becomes a quiet button, and Exclude a Folder… moves into the card's action group.
  • vaultThe two terminal rows keep their command, and the alert names what goes. This is the system's second exception: the app is about to put that line in your terminal rather than run it, so naming it describes the action.
  • removeRemove JitPass is the last card, one note and one button. Secondary, not destructive: the click opens the offboarding window, which lists everything before anything is undone.
to confirm
  • settledThe segmented filter, not the single scroll. You picked it, and the boards above are drawn as it, not as an alternative at the bottom. Protection holds what jit hands out, what it tells you and emptying the vault; Scan is the one group that reads your disk; App is how JitPass behaves here and how it leaves.
  • settledThe popup button and the switch are in the system. Both are now in windows.md › Controls and in bundle.css, with the popup drawn from the search field's tokens and its rule stated: its label is the current value, never the setting's name. The switch's rule: it applies as it is flipped and never asks, and what it costs is the row's second line, not a tooltip.
  • new, needs a wordThe dot on a pill. Nothing else in the system puts a state on a filter, because until now no filter could hide a card that needed you. It obeys the two rules that matter — 8px, and never without a word beside it. If you want it, it goes into windows.md › Controls under the segmented filter before the Swift is written; if not, the amber card has to reach you another way, and the only honest one left is opening the window on the segment that needs you.
  • roleThe eyebrow carries a group name here, not a tier. In Doctor it says which tier a card is in; in Settings it says which group, with the same dot-plus-word shape and the same state colour. If the eyebrow is meant to be a tier and nothing else, the group name moves into the card title and the dot moves beside it.
  • wording"Clean in Terminal…" or "Clean… opens your terminal". The system's example uses the longer form. The current labels are shorter and name the same surface; I kept them. Yours to pick.
  • repeatThe header says "Settings" and so does the title bar. The header region is optional, and dropping it would remove the repeat — but also the service mark, which is the one thing that says whether the two jit settings can apply at all. I kept the header. If you would rather not read the word twice, the headline can carry the service state instead.
